How the blog can bring in customers from Google

The short version: almost nobody finds staphaid.com through Google today (21 visits from search in nine months), but thousands of people a month search for the exact problems Staph Aid is built around. The biggest are recurring boils and skin infections in wrestling. A blog that answers those searches honestly, reviewed by a clinician, is the cheapest way to reach them.

1. Where the site stands now

  • 638 visits in nine months (about 70 a month), and roughly a third look like bots.
  • Google search sent 21 visits. Google Shopping’s free listings sent 36, and those visitors stayed the longest.
  • Google Analytics records no purchases at all. Either sales happen somewhere else or the tracking is broken. That needs fixing before we can show the blog made money.
  • Search Console is already set up for staphaid.com. Whoever owns it needs to add Dan so we can see the search data.

4. The rules that keep us out of trouble

  1. No treatment claims. Staph Aid is “formulated to support”. Articles about boils, MRSA at home or skin infections tell readers to see a doctor, and never present Staph Aid as a treatment.
  2. The herpes searches are for awareness only. Herpes is a virus; Staph Aid is never linked to it.
  3. Every article is reviewed by a clinician and names them, and every fact links to its source (CDC, NIH, published studies).
  4. Legal reviews anything claim-like before it goes live. That review, not the writing, will set the pace.
